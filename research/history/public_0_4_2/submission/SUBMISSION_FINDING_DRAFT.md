# Proposed October 2026 Progress Prize evidence paragraph — DRAFT ONLY

ARGUS Surface Support is an experimental open-source workflow for testing whether nonzero native-grid surface-prediction labels overlap missing *masked CT Zarr chunk objects*. The workflow preserves original source bytes, file hashes, HTTP response receipts, exact per-chunk spatial partitions, and an independent decoder. It is intended to complement, not claim priority over, existing Vesuvius phantom audits.

We first independently reproduced 11,743,996 nonzero labels across six demonstration chunks selected using historical sample outcomes. To reduce outcome-conditioned selection, we then froze a deterministic, stratified, 24-cell sample from 423 eligible prediction chunks intersecting the historical R04 halo of PHerc. 211, excluding all 18 previously inspected survey keys. The selection hash was recorded before collecting the new samples. All 24 cells and 186 unique native CT chunk states were captured in 212 uncached HTTP requests. Of the 24 cells, 13 contained stored prediction chunks and 11 did not. Two independent verification implementations agreed that the 13 stored prediction chunks contained 19,932,389 nonzero voxels, of which 8,519,612 (42.74%) mapped to locations without stored *masked* CT chunks. Five of the 13 stored prediction chunks had at least some such positives. No unsuccessful observations were discarded.

This result demonstrates a reproducible storage-support discrepancy beyond the initial hand-selected cases. It **does not** demonstrate 42.74% phantom papyrus, a scroll-wide rate, physical sheet absence, validated geometric registration, or ink/writing. It also does not independently establish a novel technical contribution relative to prior voxel-level phantom auditing. The primary proposed utility is small, fixed-sample, receipt-backed and independently verifiable data-quality audits useful during surface reconstruction evaluation.
