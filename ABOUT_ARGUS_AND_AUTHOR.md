# Why ARGUS exists

I'm **Spencer Tropea**, an independent creator in Ontario, Canada, with a background in communications and a longstanding interest in history, literature and the ways ideas survive across centuries.

I began experimenting with the Vesuvius data because the possibility of recovering ancient writing is extraordinary. Working with predicted scroll surfaces also led me to a more immediate engineering question: *what evidence actually supports a reconstruction, and how can that support be checked independently?* ARGUS developed from that question, through iterative AI-assisted coding, public research and repeated attempts to verify the original bytes rather than rely on attractive images.

The name refers to **Argus Panoptes**, the many-eyed watcher of Greek mythology. For me it suggests disciplined attention: look carefully, preserve provenance and acknowledge what remains uncertain.

These scrolls were once part of real people's intellectual lives. I sometimes think of approaching their words as **asking their permission to look**—a symbolic expression of respect, not literal authorization. They are more than a technical dataset. I hope ARGUS can contribute something practical to the collective work of studying them, and I welcome correction, independent replication and suggestions for upstream collaboration.

This is independent research, not an affiliation with the Vesuvius Challenge; I do not claim papyrological qualifications or to have invented earlier support-auditing methods.
